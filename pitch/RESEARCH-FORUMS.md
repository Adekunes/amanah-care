# Forum research note: Walidayn

Sweep date: 6 September 2026. Written for the pitch team, in support of `pitch/SCRIPT.md` Box 2 (the person) and Box 4 (the cost). This note is the follow-up to `pitch/RESEARCH-REDDIT.md`, whose Reddit sweep was blocked and produced zero verified threads.

## 1. Method

Reddit was not reachable from this environment on 6 September 2026 (see `pitch/RESEARCH-REDDIT.md` for that attempt). This sweep used other public caregiver communities instead: AgingCare.com, AlzConnected, the Alzheimer's Society UK forum (Talking Point / Dementia Support Forum), and Mumsnet (Elderly Parents, AIBU, and Chronic Pain boards) for the general caregiving angles, plus ISPU, MuslimMatters, Amaliah, and Caring.com for the Muslim-families angle.

Angles searched: siblings splitting the load unevenly, medication tracking and missed doses, the family group chat and handoffs between carers, ER visits and hospital coordination, the general mental load of caregiving, and Muslim families caring for a parent.

Every page in the findings below was re-opened directly at its exact URL before it was counted. That means: the page had to resolve to a real thread or article, not a 404 or a generic search-results listing; its title had to match the claimed title, word for word or close to it; and its actual post and reply content had to match the claimed paraphrase before the entry was kept. A page that only showed up in a search snippet, without this direct re-open and content check, was not counted.

35 pages were verified this way. Zero were dropped to the "not confirmed" appendix; every page this sweep produced held up on re-open. Because 35 is well above five, the findings below are presented as real evidence, not a placeholder.

## 2. What caregivers say, grouped by theme

### Siblings and load

Strength: 8 pages.

- One adult child ends up doing nearly all the physical care and estate work for a parent while a sibling takes vacations and only sends the occasional card. Replies tell her the resentment will not fully go away and to hire paid help from the parent's own funds rather than keep absorbing unpaid labor. (AgingCare.com, "How do you cope with anger and resentment towards a sibling who doesn't help with caregiving?", https://www.agingcare.com/questions/how-do-you-cope-with-anger-and-resentment-towards-a-sibling-who-doesnt-help-with-caregiving-486991.htm)
- A woman who has been her mother's sole Alzheimer's caregiver for seven years describes her twin sister brushing the whole situation off with "it is what it is with Mom," and grieving the sibling relationship as much as her mother's decline. (AlzConnected, "Sibling does not help nor gives support!", https://alzconnected.org/discussion/67029/sibling-does-not-help-nor-gives-support)
- Commenters describe it as common for one adult child to carry "100% of the care" for a parent with dementia while siblings stay away, often because they cannot emotionally handle watching the parent decline. (AlzConnected, "Siblings involvement in parental care", https://alzconnected.org/discussion/66246/siblings-involvement-in-parental-care)
- A woman is frustrated that her brother lives rent-free with their mother, "doesn't pay a penny" toward bills or food, and skipped a scheduled vaccination appointment for a motorcycle funeral procession. Replies warn of financial exploitation and advise tracking finances. (Alzheimer's Society UK forum, "Brother not helping with care of mum", https://forum.alzheimers.org.uk/threads/brother-not-helping-with-care-of-mum.141472/)
- A long-running, multi-page thread concludes that one sibling doing the caregiving while others "criticise and interfere without actually doing" is close to the norm on the forum, not the exception. (Alzheimer's Society UK forum, "Why do we have siblings who won't help?", https://forum.alzheimers.org.uk/threads/why-do-we-have-siblings-who-wont-help.132048/)
- A woman with her own disability handles all of her mother's shopping, appointments, and admin alone while her brother, the family's "golden child," has withdrawn completely and will not even answer their mother's calls. Replies name this a classic golden-child dynamic and push paid help instead. (Mumsnet, "Sibling not taking responsibility", https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/elderly_parents/5150771-sibling-not-taking-responsibility)
- A poster vents that her brother is "useless and selfish" about their mother's care; replies split, some pushing emotional support for the mother, one bluntly noting adult children cannot be forced into caregiving. (Mumsnet, "Mum upset at my useless and selfish brother", https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/elderly_parents/5242983-mum-upset-at-my-useless-and-selfish-brother)
- A national survey finds American Muslims caregiving for a parent split labor by gender, women doing more hands-on domestic tasks and men contributing more financially, with emotional strain hitting a majority; it does not speak to sibling conflict directly. (ISPU, "Support and Sacrifice", https://ispu.org/reports-and-analysis/support-and-sacrifice/)

### Medication

Strength: 5 pages.

- A daughter fills a weekly pill organizer for her mother but keeps finding loose pills on the floor, worried about missed doses or the family dog eating them, and gets angry pushback when she offers to hand out doses herself. (AgingCare.com, "Taking control of meds. What can I do?", https://www.agingcare.com/questions/taking-control-of-meds-what-can-i-do-463044.htm)
- A spouse caregiver asks whether a dedicated reminder app beats a phone alarm; the community answers that "nothing short of direct supervision" ensures a dose is actually taken. (AlzConnected, "Medication Reminder App Suggestions", https://alzconnected.org/discussion/66958/medication-reminder-app-suggestions)
- A caregiver describes the fatigue of endlessly re-reminding a relative about pills and appointments; one reply says handing the pills over in person beats verbal prompts, another describes an alarmed, locking dispenser as transformative. (Alzheimer's Society UK forum, "What's Your Struggle With Medication Reminders?", https://forum.alzheimers.org.uk/threads/whats-your-struggle-with-medication-reminders.167678/)
- A poster whose father's prescriptions kept changing asks how her mother can track what has already been given; replies point to printed medication charts, homemade spreadsheets, and dosette boxes. (Mumsnet, "Best way to keep track of meds given", https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/chronic_pain/5084893-best-way-to-keep-track-of-meds-given)
- A poster's mother fills a weekly pill box for the father but never actually checks whether he took the pills; replies note blister packs show what was missed but "doesn't guarantee actual consumption." (Mumsnet, "Blister packs for medication", https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/elderly_parents/5299077-blister-packs-for-medication)

### The group chat and handoffs

Strength: 6 pages.

- A person coordinating rotating agency carers for her father, with siblings who live elsewhere, asks for a handoff-pack template covering contacts, food preferences, routines, and GP info; replies suggest modeling it on a holiday-let welcome folder. (Alzheimer's Society UK forum, "carer handover", https://forum.alzheimers.org.uk/threads/carer-handover.102967/)
- A family member new to residential care is denied access to her father's digital daily notes and, missing the visibility an app at a previous facility gave her over meals, meds, and hygiene, feels reduced to phoning staff repeatedly. (Alzheimer's Society UK forum, "Daily care notes", https://forum.alzheimers.org.uk/threads/daily-care-notes.146955/)
- A poster venting in a five-person family WhatsApp group finds only one sibling ever responds, and the thread is mostly cute photos rather than real support; replies say group chats are not built for that kind of exchange. (Mumsnet, "To leave this WhatsApp group?", https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/am_i_being_unreasonable/4712994-to-leave-this-whatsapp-group)
- Among eight siblings, the two who took their mother into their home stopped sharing medical information and blocked the rest from visiting near the end of her life, despite an attorney-drafted consent form and repeated family meetings. (AgingCare.com, https://www.agingcare.com/questions/amp/464927)
- A caregiver had asked her sister for emotional support and gotten agreement, but a later venting message went unanswered, leading her to accept she would not get that support from her sibling. (AlzConnected, "Thank you and update", https://alzconnected.org/discussion/76896/thank-you-and-update)
- An article framing eldercare as a duty on every adult child describes how, in practice, care collapses onto one household while other siblings give minimal response, and urges caregivers to open the conversation about dividing support before burnout hits. (MuslimMatters, "The Sandwich Carers", https://muslimmatters.org/2026/01/14/the-sandwich-carers-navigating-the-islamic-obligation-of-eldercare/)

### The ER

Strength: 5 pages.

- A caregiver who lives minutes from her relative and would be first responder in a crisis asks how to prepare; 15 replies push a folder with ID, insurance, a current medication list, POA and advance-directive paperwork, and a POLST, since EMTs ask for these on arrival. (AgingCare.com, "How do you prepare for emergency situations?", https://www.agingcare.com/questions/how-do-you-prepare-for-emergency-situations-443551.htm)
- A poster wants a reference binder for visiting nurses; 32 replies converge on current meds with photos of each pill, allergies, provider contacts, and copies (never originals) of insurance and advance-directive documents, with warnings to keep Social Security and banking details out. (AgingCare.com, "I want to make an 'all you need to know' binder...", https://www.agingcare.com/questions/i-want-to-make-an-all-you-need-to-know-binder-for-my-elderly-parents-and-home-health-nurses-439883.htm)
- A husband describes his wife with Alzheimer's becoming so agitated in an unfamiliar ER that a doctor nearly had her involuntarily committed until a social worker stepped in; replies advise calling the primary doctor first, using urgent care when possible, and packing a go-bag. (AlzConnected, "Alzheimer/Dementia and the Emergency Room", https://alzconnected.org/discussion/74329/alzheimer-dementia-and-the-emergency-room)
- A nurse says she feels lost coordinating her father's several conditions across many separate visiting clinicians; replies flag a medication that looks wrong given his readings and suggest a geriatrician review plus a formal advance-care-planning form so any team can act without the family repeating everything. (Mumsnet, "Lost with Dads illnesses and healthcare workers", https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/elderly_parents/5515710-lost-with-dads-illnesses-and-healthcare-workers)
- A grandmother sits three days in hospital with no diagnosis and no callback, until a relative who is herself a physician calls; an MRI is then ordered and finds a stroke that had been mistaken for vertigo. (AgingCare.com, "How can I help my grandmother who is in the hospital without a diagnosis?", https://www.agingcare.com/questions/help-grandmother-in-hospital-doesnt-know-diagnosis-150052.htm)

### The mental load

Strength: 6 pages.

- An only child managing her mother's finances, house, and pets on top of a full-time job describes total exhaustion after 18 months; replies from other only children push firm boundaries and outside help rather than absorbing everything solo. (AgingCare.com, "Any only children experiencing caregiver burnout?", https://www.agingcare.com/questions/only-children-experiencing-caregiver-burnout-178977.htm)
- A woman whose father moved in full-time after her mother's death describes exhaustion from his repeated demands and feeling unsupported by a sister who offers no help even from a distance; she ends up committing to a support group and weekly respite time. (AlzConnected, "Caregiver Burn Out", https://alzconnected.org/discussion/76883/caregiver-burn-out)
- A daughter already holding power of attorney describes the disorienting shift from "daughter" to "primary caregiver" after her father's early-onset diagnosis; the community's advice is to lock down legal paperwork first, then claim even five minutes of self-care daily. (AlzConnected, "Switching from daughter to caregiver...", https://alzconnected.org/discussion/76700/switching-from-daughter-to-caregiver-resources-advice-anything)
- A mother raising two children while also overseeing her own mother's care is alarmed that an evening carer skipped medication and she only found out from a call from the GP; replies push her to register power of attorney formally and keep pressing for direct notification, calling that legitimate oversight rather than "rocking the boat." (Alzheimer's Society UK forum, "Sandwich generation", https://forum.alzheimers.org.uk/threads/sandwich-generation.119366/)
- A woman recovering from a health crisis and a job loss visits her parents four or five times a week because her father refuses to pay for care despite having the money; the thread's overwhelming response is that she must start saying no. (Mumsnet, "I am finding helping out elderly and unwell parents is utterly, utterly draining...", https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/elderly_parents/5314624-i-am-finding-helping-out-elderly-and-unwell-parents-is-utterly-utterly-draining-and-ruining-my-life-as-it-currently-stands)
- A woman living abroad who arranges banking, bills, and a panic button for her mother during visits is repeatedly accused by an aunt of not doing enough, while her brother nearby faces no such criticism; commenters name this a gendered double standard. (Mumsnet, "Getting Shamed for Elderly Care", https://www.mumsnet.com/talk/elderly_parents/5243104-getting-shamed-for-elderly-care)

### Muslim families

Strength: 5 pages tagged directly to this angle (plus the two Muslim-content pages already listed above under Siblings and load, and Group chat and handoffs, for 7 pages total that speak to Muslim caregiving).

- A national poll finds Muslims are more likely than any other group surveyed to be caring for an aging parent, and that this burden peaks for people aged 30 to 49 who are also raising school-age children. (ISPU, "Sandwiched", https://ispu.org/muslim-families/)
- A reader worries her elderly mother is drifting from regular prayer; the advice columnist counsels gentle, non-judgmental conversation and leaning on community elders rather than direct pressure. (Amaliah, "Agony Aunt: Convincing an Elderly Parent to Be a More Practicing Muslim", https://www.amaliah.com/post/68994/agony-aunt-convincing-elderly-parent-practicing-muslim)
- A profile of Sydney's first Muslim aged-care home describes halal meals, prayer rooms, qibla-facing beds, and multilingual staff, framed as a response to the stigma that placing a parent in care means abandoning them. (Amaliah, "This Is the First Muslim Care Home for the Elderly in Australia", https://www.amaliah.com/post/51536/first-muslim-care-home-elderly-australia-gallipoli-home-care-home-elderly-muslim-sydney)
- A caregiver-facing guide lists what to check when placing a Muslim elder in assisted living: same-gender aides, halal kitchens, on-site prayer space, mosque transport for Friday prayers, and advice on easing the guilt of placement. (Caring.com, "Assisted Living for Muslim Seniors", https://www.caring.com/resources/find-assisted-living-for-muslim-seniors)
- A widely read piece counsels that caring for elderly parents is often exhausting in practice, and that caregivers should keep their composure gentle under stress, tying patience directly to Quranic instruction on filial duty. (MuslimMatters, "The Etiquette of Dealing with Parents and the Elderly", https://muslimmatters.org/2011/12/09/yasir-qadhi-the-etiquette-of-dealing-with-parents-and-the-elderly/)

## 3. Pain point table

| Pain point | What Walidayn does today | Gap or next step |
|---|---|---|
| One sibling carries almost all the caregiving while others do little (8 sibling threads) | Home dashboard shows "the last seven days and who carried the week," visible to the whole family without a confrontation | No gentle way to loop in a barely-involved sibling short of full onboarding; the imbalance is visible but nothing nudges anyone |
| Families fill pill organizers but never confirm a dose was actually taken (AgingCare, Mumsnet blister-pack thread) | Two-tap meds logging with undo; a retracted entry stays struck through and drops out of counts instead of being deleted | No photo or verification step that distinguishes "dispensed" from "confirmed taken" |
| No app substitutes for physically watching someone take their pills (AlzConnected) | Logging captures the human's own observation as the event of record | The app cannot replace supervision; it can only record who supervised and when |
| Group chats bury real updates under photos and go unanswered when it matters (Mumsnet WhatsApp thread, AlzConnected "Thank you and update") | Dashboard, Record, and handoff cards replace the chat feed with a computed, structured record instead of a scroll to search | Core value proposition already; remaining risk is getting a skeptical relative off the group chat and onto the app at all |
| Families build ad hoc "binder" folders of meds, allergies, POA, and insurance for nurses and EMTs (two AgingCare threads, emergency-prep thread) | Hospital sheet compiles fourteen days of the log in one tap | No place to store the standing documents themselves (POA, advance directive, insurance card, allergy list) alongside the log |
| Families are shut out of a facility's own daily notes and reduced to phoning staff (Alzheimer's Society "Daily care notes") | Support-worker login (blue view) puts agency staff into the same shared record the family sees | Consent filter is UI-only today per the README's own honest limits; a facility's separate system outside Walidayn is not covered |
| A caregiver asks a sibling for emotional support and gets silence when it counts (AlzConnected) | Out of scope by design: Walidayn is a coordination record, not a support or therapy tool | No in-app path to a support group or counseling referral; could link out without becoming a new product |
| Muslim families want halal, prayer-aware, same-gender care and worry placement means abandonment (Caring.com, Amaliah, MuslimMatters) | Cultural notes on stored preferences ride on top of every card and every recommendation | No directory of vetted Muslim-friendly agencies or facilities; the agency-pays side of the business could fund this later |
| Solo and only-child caregivers hit burnout and are told to set firm boundaries and get outside help (AgingCare, Mumsnet "utterly draining") | Recommendation rules flag one person carrying 70% of the week, no logging for 48 hours, and similar patterns, each with a plain "because" line and role-specific actions | Recommendations are counts-only, never a diagnosis, and stop short of linking to an actual respite or day-program booking |

## 5. Top gaps

Ranked by how often the forums ask for it, with a one-line effort estimate.

1. **Store the standing documents, not just the log.** Two separate AgingCare threads and the emergency-prep thread all converge on a binder of POA, insurance, allergy list, and advance directive, kept alongside the meds and routine. Effort: medium, an encrypted-attachment field on the family record and a slot on the hospital sheet.
2. **Confirm a dose was taken, not just dispensed.** Both medication threads say no reminder tool beats watching the pill actually go down. Effort: low to medium, add a "confirmed taken" sub-status to the existing meds category rather than a new feature.
3. **Real push notifications, not a poll.** The facility "Daily care notes" thread and the WhatsApp thread both turn on someone missing an update because they were not looking at the right screen at the right moment. Effort: medium to high; already named as a roadmap item in the README.
4. **A low-stakes way to loop in a barely-involved sibling.** Eight separate sibling threads describe a sibling who will not fully engage; a full onboarding ask may be why they never show up at all. Effort: medium, a view-only digest or link that asks nothing of the recipient beyond reading it.
5. **A resource link for burnout and disengaged-sibling grief.** Several threads end with the community pointing the caregiver toward a support group or respite program because the sibling conflict itself will not resolve. Effort: low, a static, curated resource list, not a new product.
6. **A directory of Muslim-friendly agencies and facilities.** Caring.com and Amaliah both describe families hunting for halal, prayer-aware, same-gender care one facility at a time. Effort: high, needs partnerships and vetting, a natural fit for the agency-pays side of the business rather than this pilot.

### Ranked list
- Store the standing documents (POA, insurance, allergy list, advance directive), not just the daily log: medium effort, an encrypted attachment field plus a slot on the hospital sheet.
- Confirm a dose was actually taken, not just dispensed: low to medium effort, a status flag on the existing meds category.
- Real push notifications instead of a poll: medium to high effort, already on the README roadmap.
- A low-stakes, view-only way to loop in a barely-involved sibling: medium effort, no full onboarding required.
- A resource link for burnout and disengaged-sibling grief: low effort, a static curated list.
- A directory of Muslim-friendly agencies and facilities: high effort, partnerships and vetting, a fit for the agency-pays business line.

## 6. Limits

These communities skew US and UK. AgingCare.com, AlzConnected, and Mumsnet are all English-language, and the Alzheimer's Society forum is explicitly UK-based; none of this sweep reached a Canadian or Montreal-specific community, even though the pilot in Box 6 is Montreal. Posters on these forums are self-selected: people write in when they are mid-crisis, venting, or looking for validation, so the sweep likely overstates how dramatic the average family's conflict is, not how common milder versions of it are. Several of the strongest threads (AlzConnected, much of the Alzheimer's Society forum) are dementia-specific communities, and dementia caregiving is heavier and different in kind from the day-to-day logistics Walidayn targets for a broader elder-care family. The Muslim-families angle is thinner and more editorial than the other five: most of it comes from published articles and advice columns (ISPU, Amaliah, Caring.com, MuslimMatters) rather than live forum threads with replies, so it says less about how a Muslim family's own siblings actually divide the load day to day. This sweep cannot prove market size, cannot prove Muslim families in particular feel sibling resentment the way the general-forum threads describe it, and cannot stand in for the five-family pilot's own data.
